# Scratchpad and Archive
Organize exploration so current work is easy to find and its history remains browsable. Apply moves within the requested scope and the ownership boundaries in [Pages and agent boundaries](pages.md).

## Purpose
Scratchpad holds active exploration: alternatives, rough ideas, interaction studies, flow sketches and work that is not yet integrated into Workshop. It need not be organized entirely around formal design questions. Group work by a question when that helps; a topic, experiment or loose collection is also valid. Give each group a meaningful title without inventing a decision the work has not reached.

Archive preserves a browsable history of exploration that no longer needs to occupy the active Scratchpad. Create it when there is material to file away. Named versions and repository exports supplement that visible history; they do not replace it.

Workshop holds the connected work-in-progress prototype. Selecting an option in Scratchpad does not by itself integrate it into Workshop.

## Organization and presentation
Use the native section or board title once. Add a short explanation only when the title is insufficient; do not repeat it as a decorative banner. Keep related material together with enough separation to distinguish groups.

When comparing alternatives, label them A, B, C or with equally clear names. Use comparable content, starting conditions and viewport where practical so differences in the approach remain easy to judge. Show enough context to understand what happens before and after the interaction without building an entire prototype for every experiment.

Keep alternatives distinct from successive states within an alternative. Within a flow, arrange progression left to right and branches on separate rows. Label the group so a reader can distinguish a comparison of options from a sequence of steps. Loose sketches need not imitate a complete flow.

## Explicit status
Give each coherent group a visible status. Apply status at the group level unless individual options genuinely differ; do not repeat it on every frame merely for uniformity.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Open | Exploration is active; no direction has been selected. |
| Chosen — awaiting integration | A direction has been selected, but it is not yet incorporated and checked in the connected Workshop routes. |
| Integrated | The chosen behavior is incorporated and checked in the relevant Workshop routes. Link to that implementation. |
| Parked | The work is deliberately inactive, without implying approval or rejection. |

These are useful defaults, not a mandatory linear process. A loose study can remain Open, work can be revisited, and not every exploration needs a winning option. Identify a superseded approach and link to its successor when there is one. Do not infer selection, rejection or approval from where a board happens to sit.

## Integration
Carry the chosen approach into the relevant connected Workshop routes and shared components within the authorized scope. Check the affected states, entry points and exits rather than leaving it as a standalone demonstration. Record remaining work in the partner repository. Mark the exploration Integrated only when that work has been completed and checked; selection alone is insufficient.

## Browsable history
Move older, inactive exploration to Archive when it makes the active Scratchpad easier to use. There is no age threshold or automatic purge. A useful recent comparison may remain in Scratchpad; archived work remains available to revisit.

Preserve coherent groups with their labels, status, useful context and links to decisions or successors. Make the original owner clear in each archived group, retaining authorship context where it differs. Use topic and date labels where they aid retrieval, without imposing a numbering scheme for archive groups.

Before a move, check references, prototype connections and component dependencies. Repair links affected by the move and verify the retained work is readable. If the tool cannot preserve necessary behavior across pages, retain the working source and use a clearly labelled archival copy or snapshot with a source link. Archival does not authorize deleting work or editing someone else's owned material.

Treat Archive as historical evidence, not current design guidance. Follow links to Workshop, Prototype and maintained repository records to establish the current design. To resume an archived exploration, bring a working copy into Scratchpad and link it back to the historical group.

## Canvas and repository
The canvas carries the title or question, option labels, brief differences, status and useful links. It should be understandable without opening a separate document for every small detail.

The partner repository carries decisions, rationale, unresolved work and verification. Link the exploration and its repository record in both directions where useful. An orientation board points to those records rather than maintaining a second task list. Keep private research in its appropriate location; do not copy it into a public repository for organizational convenience.

## Page conventions
Archive is a shared page at the end of the file, after Component Library and any owned working pages. Create it when there is historical material to hold. Each group identifies its original owner; sharing the page does not remove ownership boundaries for its contents.
