# Change Showroom deliberately with provenance
Showroom tells the design's story to a particular audience. It is curated, not generated, and its form is still being discovered: let real walkthroughs, their audiences and the material they draw on shape it rather than a fixed template.

- **Change it deliberately.** Showroom changes only when someone is working on it at the user's request, never automatically. Preserve existing slides and their wording unless the user asks to change them.
- **Draw on the real material.** A walkthrough can draw on recorded and transcribed feedback, meeting and session notes, strategy discussions with collaborators or agents, research documents and the wireframes themselves. Note where each idea comes from.
- **Cite, never invent.** Cite each source by name, linking to it where possible. Never invent a source, quote or finding, and keep what sources say separate from design proposals.
- **Show screens as marked copies.** A screen shown in Showroom is a copy from Prototype, labelled with the Prototype screen it came from and linked to it, so people can click through for themselves.
- **Label what is not current.** Workshop, Scratchpad or Archive material appears only when the story needs it, marked as a proposal, option or historical exploration as appropriate.
- **Learn from use.** When a way of presenting works well, record it in the project, so the approach grows from real walkthroughs.

## Authority and procedures
Apply the relevant wireframing skill for the task procedure. Neither this policy nor a skill grants permission to change a design file, another owner's work, tools or external systems. Preserve the authorization and ownership limits in the agreement and the actual project instructions. Report a substantive conflict for human resolution rather than silently changing the agreement or treating a successful structural check as acceptance.
